# Torch safety controls

LightSync lighting uses a phone torch when supported, with synchronized full-screen light as a fallback. Audience screens stay dark unless the guest chooses screen output or torch control is unavailable. A phone must explicitly join and accept the photosensitivity notice. The torch-control camera track is used only to switch the flashlight. It is not displayed, read, stored, or uploaded; LightSync has no audience video recording feature. Screen output needs no camera permission.

Enhanced accessibility protection is enabled for every new room. It caps a pattern at 1.5 device-light on-edges per second, accounting for the number of pulses in that pattern. Turning it off requires the controller to acknowledge this warning:

> We have zero control over what may happen. If you are sensitive to light, stop using/participating and seek a room without flashing rapidly lights.

The pre-show calibration uses one light pulse per second and a generated browser beep; it does not use or record microphone audio. The warning explains that phone torches are binary and cannot be dimmed; screen output uses the controller's selected colors. The local music cue designer encodes up to 1,800 discrete beat keyframes over ten minutes. Each keyframe’s selected pulse gesture and color pass through the audience device’s existing protection governor; very fast beats can be skipped. Disabling enhanced protection never disables the fixed safeguard: no more than three rising device-light edges in any rolling second, with a minimum 333 ms gap. The audience browser independently applies the governor and can always turn its output off. Stop, pause, blackout, leave, hidden-page suspension, disconnect, or room end requests light off and stops the torch-control camera track where applicable. Blackout bypasses ordinary command throttling and cancels calibration.

Audio-generated light scores use discrete beat keyframes, not a continuous BPM pulse. The same audience-side governor applies to every keyframe; at faster tempos, it can suppress hits that exceed the selected protection limit. Torch hardware and browser scheduling vary. The app cannot guarantee a particular exposure, hardware shutdown, medical outcome, or accessibility for an individual. The controller and audience page show the risk notice and opt-out controls; organizers should announce the effect and follow venue guidance.
